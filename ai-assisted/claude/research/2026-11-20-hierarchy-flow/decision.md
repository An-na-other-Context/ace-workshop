## The question (annaothercrow)

Which visual hierarchy and flow choices reduce barriers in cards, pages and chat replies? Depth 2: what others tried, suggested and concluded. Source areas Anna asked for: studies with people with similar traits, SIGACCESS (ASSETS), neurodiversity-affirming design, critical disability theory and studies, co-design and participatory design with neurodivergent people, COGA in full, working design systems, dyslexia style guides.

## Decision (Anna (@annaothercrow), 20.11.2026): all as a trial

### Cards

- A purpose line: what the card is for, why, and its size in effort (F2, F15, F23).
- At most 5 choices; extras in a fold whose label says what's inside (F20, F26).
- Icons only beside words, few, never decorative (F1, F19). This replaces "an icon on every label" from earlier the same day.
- Skip and Pause as big as Send (F9; open question E4).
- The "Do" line at the top: tested side by side, "Do: ① tap ② write ③ Send" and "Step: … · One option: …" (E1; F35–F38).

### Pages

- Dyslexia Scotland's values: lines 50–75 characters, line spacing 1.5, left-aligned, key information first (F29).
- Only extras folded; what most readers need stays open (F26).
- Emphasis by size and weight; no italics, underline or all caps (F28).
- A short / full switch the reader sets (E6).

### Chat replies

- Answer or action first (F18, F29).
- Short headings and numbered steps; bullets only inside a step (F31, F33).
- Fewer hedges (F31).
- The reply shape, asked once: headed steps, "✓ Done · → Next · ? Open" (F33).

## What the research shows (AI summary of research.html)

- Less, one at a time: one step or question, about five choices, minimal design, clear headings, a short main path (F1, F17, F20, F21, F25).
- Icons beside words, few. Autistic students preferred text over visuals. No source found says more icons, colour or emphasis help (F1, F19).
- Saying what a step is for, why, and how big helps people start (F2, F15, F23).
- The setting adjusts, not the reader: density the reader sets; skip and pause that keep you included (F8–F10).
- Demand pressure is untested: no study tests action bars, badges or counters with PDA readers. Guidance favours choice and fewer demands; describing instead of instructing helps some people and burdens others (F35–F38).
- Thin direct evidence: one co-design study with autistic students, read in part; most advice comes from guidelines (F7). 22 of 39 sources were seen only as summaries.

## Trade-offs accepted

- Anna's wish for "more symbols and graphics" became "icons only beside words" (F1, F19). Anna picked it.
- The imperative "Do:" line stays in a side-by-side test, though PDA guidance leans to choice wording (E1).
- The rules rest mostly on guidelines and one partly read study, not on tests with people with the learner profile's traits.
