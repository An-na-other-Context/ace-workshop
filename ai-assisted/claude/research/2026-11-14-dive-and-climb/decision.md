## The question (annaothercrow)

Which learning approaches match "dive and climb" (go down layer by layer asking why, when, where, who, what and how; check "still on track?"; climb back up, applying each layer)? What do they need to work, and where do they go wrong?

## What the research shows (AI summary of research.html)

- No published method covers the whole loop. The closest is "semantic waves", which even names the risk of going down and never climbing back up, the "down escalator" (A1).
- Asking why and explaining to yourself help, but asking why is weak where little is known yet, which is every new bottom layer; picturing it or a concrete example may work better there (A11, N4).
- Exploring with guidance and a summing-up step works; exploring without help does worse (A9, N1). Help should shrink as a layer is mastered (N2).
- Research with autistic, ADHD and dyslexic learners mostly fits the model and names the risks: transitions, getting stuck, interruptions, and a whole picture that forms more slowly (P1–P12).
- Most sources were seen only as summaries (43 of 66), so these points rest on thinner reading than the workshop research.

## What it could mean for ACE Workshop (AI interpretation, to check)

- The climb back up has to be built into each layer, not left to chance.
- The "still on track?" check works better from inside the task than as an interruption.
- Layer boundaries can work as save points; new layers need more help than familiar ones.
