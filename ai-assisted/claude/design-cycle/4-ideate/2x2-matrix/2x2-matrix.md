# 2x2 matrix

**Phase 4 · Ideate** · a tool of the [design cycle](../../README.md)

> **AI-made.** Drafted by Claude (AI) from Anna (@annaothercrow)'s solo design-thinking toolkit, which adapts *The Design Thinking Toolbox* for one person. The choices are Anna's. See [Who made what](#who-made-what) and [Sources](#sources).

Board version: [2x2-matrix.excalidraw](2x2-matrix.excalidraw). Open it in Excalidraw or in VS Code.

A 2x2 matrix is a grid made of two axes, which gives four corners. It needs no [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md#where-it-does-not-apply), because it already is a criteria matrix: its two axes are the criteria, named before any card moves.

## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 155–157):

- **Goal:** sort and prioritise ideas, or spot chances and patterns.
- **What it is:** a grid with two axes you choose. Any meaningful pair works: high and low, or two opposites. It turns up in every phase, wherever a decision gets made.
- **Use it to:** see fast which ideas to follow and which to drop · get an overview of ideas that already have some shape · prioritise by strategy, by chances or by any other category · make a decision visible.
- **Axis pairs the book lists:** cool ↔ normal · feasible ↔ impossible · difficult ↔ simple · important ↔ negligible · costs ↔ savings · benefit ↔ no need · effort ↔ simple · impact ↔ negative impact · value ↔ no value · innovation ↔ existing · ambivalent ↔ specific. Its drawing sets value (high ↔ low) against trouble (little ↔ much).
- **Why the book's expert picks it** (p. 157): you can pick any axes, so it maps anything from a small technical decision to a whole concept. It is simple, and strong to look at.

## The cycle, alone

1. **Name the axes.** Write both pairs down before placing any card. Early on: cool against doable.
2. **Place the cards.** Rough corner first, then next to the others. A card that won't sit anywhere holds two thoughts: split it.
3. **Read the grid.** The top right goes forward, up to three cards. An empty corner is a finding in itself.
4. **Clarity check.** All five pass → the top-right cards go to [NABC](../nabc/nabc.md) (need, approach, benefit, competition). Not yet, or the picture says nothing → back to 1: change an axis and keep the same cards.

No fixed time (Anna, 19.09.2026). Place one cluster at a time; the placing itself is quick. Stopping is allowed at any step.

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 157–158)

- **One tool for many jobs:** technical decisions, business models, concepts. Alone: your own subjects, such as ideas, tools, futures or problems.
- **Strategic planning:** map current uses and chances, and see which areas no idea covers yet. Alone: map the tools you already use and see which part of a run nothing covers.
- **Specific, measurable axes** also make it useful in Prototype. Alone: axes you can actually judge; *doable this week* is easier to judge than *doable*.
- **Cool and doable for a quick early judgement:** the coolest idea may not be doable yet, and the pair asks whether there is any way it could be. Alone: it keeps a shiny idea visible without letting it take over.
- **Break a complex idea into its parts before placing it.** A card that won't sit is two cards.
- **Use case:** early on the team uses cool against doable, and closer to a solution, impact against expenditure. Ideas in the weak corner go to the parking lot, and when the team gets stuck, the parking lot is a good place to look for inspiration. Alone: the same parking lot.
- **Key learnings:** keep the ideas as simple as possible · rewrite a card or split it when that makes the placing clearer · not every pair of axes gives useful results, so experiment and fit the axes to the problem. Alone: when the picture says nothing, change an axis and keep the ideas.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Dot voting** (p. 159), an alternative: criteria first, then dots. Not built here; see Parked below.
- **Kano classification** (no page), an alternative: sorts features into delighters, performance features and basics. Named only; not built here.
- **Brainstorming** (p. 151), what comes before: the round that makes the cards. [Brainstorming](../brainstorming/brainstorming.md).
- **Design principles** (p. 53), a helper: guidelines that frame decisions; they can become axes. Not built here.
- **Define success** (p. 137), a helper: what success means, on two timelines; it gives honest axes. [Define success](../../3-define/define-success/define-success.md).
- **Venn diagrams** (p. 20), a helper: feasibility, economic viability and desirability as three overlapping circles. Not opened for the toolkit; not built here.

## Parked: dot voting (p. 159)

Above eight people the book suggests dot voting instead: criteria agreed first, then everyone places dots on the options. It needs a group, so it stays parked (Anna, 19.09.2026). The [Problem statement](../../1-understand/problem-statement/problem-statement.md) explains it in full. Alone, the axes do the choosing.
