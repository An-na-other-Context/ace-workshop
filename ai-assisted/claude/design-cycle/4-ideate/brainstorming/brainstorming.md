# Brainstorming

**Phase 4 · Ideate** · a tool of the [design cycle](../../README.md)

> **AI-made.** Drafted by Claude (AI) from Anna (@annaothercrow)'s solo design-thinking toolkit, which adapts *The Design Thinking Toolbox* for one person. The choices are Anna's. See [Who made what](#who-made-what) and [Sources](#sources).

Board version: [brainstorming.excalidraw](brainstorming.excalidraw). Open it in Excalidraw or in VS Code.

## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 151–153):

- **Goal:** lots of ideas, fast. How many matters more than how good.
- **What it is:** everyone adds what they know, at whatever level, and every idea is welcome. A first session often empties the heads (a "brain dump", p. 151, quoted), so later sessions can focus on the problem.
- **The nine rules** (p. 152): creative confidence · quantity before quality · visual ideas · use gestures · build on the ideas of others · one person speaks at a time · no prejudices · keep going · fail often and early.
- **Use it to:** get many ideas and variants in a short time · use a team's whole creative range · gather views from different backgrounds.
- **Why the book's expert picks it** (p. 153): it shows what a team and each person in it can do, and it keeps ideas alive. Teams often come back to an idea that first looked impossible.
- **A parking lot of its own** (p. 151): a place for ideas that sound exciting but don't help with the problem right now.

## The cycle, alone

1. **The question.** Write it at the top, word for word, from the [How might we](../../3-define/how-might-we/how-might-we.md) sheet.
2. **Write, don't judge.** One idea per line, in words or sketches. Stop when the ideas thin out.
3. **Change the question.** A new angle starts a new round of step 2: what if it cost nothing? what would make it worse? Ideas still coming → another round. Ideas thin → go on to 4.
4. **Cluster, then walk the matrix.** Group what belongs together, then walk the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md) over the clusters. What doesn't go forward goes to the parking lot.
5. **Clarity check.** All five pass → the forward clusters go to the [2x2 matrix](../2x2-matrix/2x2-matrix.md). Not yet → back to 3 for another round, or to the step an open check points at.

Short bursts, with no clock as a whip. A timebox is only for measuring an expectation, never pressure for its own sake (Anna, 18.09.2026). Stopping is allowed at any step.

### 4 · Cluster, then walk the matrix (out loud)

- Cluster: ___________ · ideas ___
- Cluster: ___________ · ideas ___
- Cluster: ___________ · ideas ___

Ask each cluster these four questions, after the rounds and never during one. They are the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md), reworded for idea clusters:

1. A new idea, or one that keeps coming back? → goes forward if: keeps coming back
2. Would building it add value right now? → goes forward if: yes
3. Can I try it with what I have? → goes forward if: doable
4. Does it get costlier if I don't try it? → goes forward if: costlier

- Cluster ___: 1 ___ · 2 ___ · 3 ___ · 4 ___ → forward / parking lot

The matrix sorts; no idea gets criticised.

### 5 · Clarity check

- [ ] 1 · A written question at the top: the round has one clear question
- [ ] 2 · Nothing judged during the round: judging waits for the matrix
- [ ] 3 · Clustered afterwards: similar ideas grouped together
- [ ] 4 · The sheet is saved: photographed or filed before you close it
- [ ] 5 · Clear to me tomorrow: I'd understand the ideas without an explanation

All five pass → the forward clusters go to the 2x2 matrix. Not yet → back to 3 for another round, or to the step an open check points at.

## The nine rules, alone (p. 152)

| # | The book's rule | Alone |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Creative confidence | Holds. The first idea doesn't have to be good; that's what the round is for. |
| 2 | Quantity before quality | Holds. Count the ideas; their worth comes later. |
| 3 | Visual ideas | Holds. A sketch counts as an idea. |
| 4 | Use gestures | A group rule; it falls away. Saying an idea out loud can still help thinking. |
| 5 | Build on the ideas of others | Changes: build on your own earlier ideas, or on a borrowed voice (figuring storming, in [Special brainstorming](../special-brainstorming/special-brainstorming.md)). |
| 6 | One person speaks at a time | A group rule; it falls away. |
| 7 | No prejudices | Holds, about your own ideas too. |
| 8 | Keep going | Holds. When it thins out, change the question instead of pushing. |
| 9 | Fail often and early | Iterate often instead of fixing what doesn't work for you. Never "I failed". (Anna, 18.09.2026) |

## The clarity checks, and where they come from

| Check | What the book says |
|---|---|
| 1 · A written question at the top | Prepare one clear question for the session: a How might we question, or one that asks what possibilities there are. (p. 152) |
| 2 · Nothing judged during the round | Never criticise an idea, since you may need it again later; leave out any rating during the session. (p. 153, 154) |
| 3 · Clustered afterwards | Cluster and assess the ideas at regular intervals. (p. 152) |
| 4 · The sheet is saved | Photograph the walls of sticky notes; they get lost easily while everyone is busy. (p. 153) |
| 5 · Clear to me tomorrow | No book source: the check Anna chose for Problem statement and Jobs to be done (Anna, 19.09.2026). |

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 153–154)

- **Lots of ideas matter more than one perfect idea.** Never criticise one; you might need it later. Alone the same: the matrix sorts afterwards and nothing gets thrown away.
- **The book says a session shows how people work under time pressure.** Time pressure is on Anna's no list (Anna, 18.09.2026). What stays: short bursts and changed questions.
- **Photograph the walls.** Save the sheet before closing it.
- **Linked ideas often make very good solutions.** Look for pairs that combine.
- **Timing:** a session early in the day works well, or after a break or a warm-up. Alone: your own timing; a warm-up counts.
- **Use case:** the team prefers the structured variant: everyone writes first, no moderator is needed and everyone gets an equal chance. Alone that's the default. The team builds on each other's ideas; alone, the second round builds on your own.
- **Key learnings:** keep the atmosphere calm and relaxed (alone: a quiet room, with nothing waiting to interrupt) · accept every idea (clarity check 2) · use it in several phases, as a first emptying of heads and later to improve things (it is also the idea test on the How might we sheet).

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **How might we** (p. 125), the entry: the question a session starts from. [How might we](../../3-define/how-might-we/how-might-we.md).
- **Brainwriting 6-3-5** (p. 163), an alternative: six people each write three ideas and pass the sheet on five times, in silence. Not built here; parked in Anna's toolkit because it runs on other people's sheets (Anna, 20.09.2026).
- **Special brainstorming** (p. 167), an alternative for when a round goes in circles: [Special brainstorming](../special-brainstorming/special-brainstorming.md).
- **Analogies & benchmarking** (p. 171), an alternative: other worlds that solved the same pain. [Analogies & benchmarking](../analogies-and-benchmarking/analogies-and-benchmarking.md).
- **2x2 matrix** (p. 155), what comes after: sort the ideas on two axes. [2x2 matrix](../2x2-matrix/2x2-matrix.md).

## Parked: the group rules (p. 152)

Two of the nine rules only work with other people in the room: use gestures, and one person speaks at a time. Alone they fall away. The structured variant's sharing round, where each person puts up and explains their notes, has nobody to share with either; its write-first half stays as the default.
