## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 171, 172, 175):

- **Goal:** find inspiration in fields that seem to have nothing to do with your problem.
- **What it is:** another industry, animals, people or organisations serve as a benchmark (a point of comparison) or an analogy, to change how you approach the problem. Mostly used in Ideate; sometimes earlier, while the problem is still being defined.
- **Use it to:** find ideas that surprise · explain complex things in a way people understand · compare your problem with one already solved elsewhere · work on open, badly structured problems · with sketch notes, unfold more creativity.
- **The book's example** (p. 172): a shoe whose pain is low status, and how the car industry solved status. Those solutions then turn into ideas for the shoe.
- **Why the book's expert picks it** (p. 175): it completes a normal brainstorm, and analogies combined with sketch notes are fun both to make and to look at.

## The cycle, alone

1. **Name the pain.** One critical experience, written first, from the [Jobs to be done](../../1-understand/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done.md) or [Customer journey map](../../2-observe/customer-journey-map/customer-journey-map.md) sheet.
2. **Find worlds.** Where is this pain already solved? Other fields, nature, other countries, and one world far away from yours.
3. **Say how they solved it.** Write down the mechanism that did it.
4. **Transfer, then sort.** Carry the solution back to your problem and sketch it. Then walk the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md) over the transferred ideas.
5. **Clarity check.** All five pass → forward to the [2x2 matrix](../2x2-matrix/2x2-matrix.md) or an [NABC](../nabc/nabc.md) (need, approach, benefit, competition). Not yet, or nothing surprising yet → back to 2 for a further world, or to 1 for the next pain.

No fixed time (Anna, 19.09.2026). One pain per round. Stopping is allowed at any step.

### 4 · Walk the matrix (out loud) over the transferred ideas

A borrowed idea can look shinier than it is. Ask each one these four questions. They are the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md), reworded for transferred ideas:

1. A new idea, or one that keeps coming back? → goes forward if: keeps coming back
2. Would building it add value right now? → goes forward if: yes
3. Can I try it with what I have? → goes forward if: doable
4. Does it get costlier if I don't try it? → goes forward if: costlier

- A___: 1 ___ · 2 ___ · 3 ___ · 4 ___ → forward / parking lot

### 5 · Clarity check

- [ ] 1 · The pain is named first: before any world is searched
- [ ] 2 · Each analogy names how that world solved it: the mechanism, written down
- [ ] 3 · One far-away world among them: beyond the close neighbours
- [ ] 4 · Each solution transferred back, and sketched: drawn as well as named
- [ ] 5 · Clear to me tomorrow: I'd understand the board without an explanation

All five pass → forward to the 2x2 matrix or an NABC. Not yet → back to 2 for a further world, or to 1 for the next pain.

## The sketch-note variation (p. 173)

Split a large sheet into three columns: attributes · analogies · ideas. List the attributes of the problem, find an analogy for each, then solve the problem from the analogy's point of view as a sketch note, and grow the ideas from there. This is the variation that allows working alone. [AEIOU](../../2-observe/aeiou/aeiou.md) (activities, environment, interactions, objects, users) can supply the attributes.

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 175–176)

- **Focus on the critical experiences and pains:** that's where new and intriguing angles show up. Alone: the pains are on the Jobs to be done and journey sheets.
- **Look at absurd industries and concepts too.** Fields far from your own often help most, even when at first they seem to share nothing. Alone: keep at least one far world (check 3).
- **When nothing comes, search images for two attributes**, for example fast and comfortable, and thousands of objects show up. Alone: allowed under the research rule, with sources named and blocked sites listed.
- **Practise daily:** look for similarities while watching TV or on the way somewhere. Alone: cheap and pleasant, no sheet needed.
- **Borrowing is allowed:** whenever a problem comes up, ask how other people solved it. Alone: games have solved many of these pains already.
- **Use case:** after an intense session the team's thoughts went to zoos, aliens, strange planets and off-road expeditions; the decisive sparks came from culture, from nature, and from understanding new circumstances. Alone: let the far-away worlds stand; that's where the surprise is.
- **Key learnings:** analogies and benchmarks add a lot to any ideation · they often find approaches that experts in their own field had overlooked · analogies combined with sketches raise creativity. Alone: your own field has blind spots too, and a sketched analogy goes further than a named one.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Brainstorming** (p. 151), an alternative: quantity before quality, with nine rules. [Brainstorming](../brainstorming/brainstorming.md).
- **Special brainstorming** (p. 167), an alternative: negative brainstorming, figuring storming, bodystorming. [Special brainstorming](../special-brainstorming/special-brainstorming.md).
- **Brainwriting 6-3-5** (p. 163), an alternative: six people each write three ideas and pass the sheet on five times, in silence. Not built here.
- **How might we** (p. 125), a helper: the question the search runs under. [How might we](../../3-define/how-might-we/how-might-we.md).
- **AEIOU** (p. 107), a helper: activities, environment, interactions, objects, users; it also gives attributes to search analogies for. [AEIOU](../../2-observe/aeiou/aeiou.md).
- **Sketch notes** (p. 173–174), the book's variation: three columns, with the analogy's solution drawn. The [sketch card](../../supports/sketch/sketch.md).
