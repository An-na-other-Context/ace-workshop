## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 167, 169):

- **Goal:** many unusual ideas in a short time.
- **What it is:** three alternatives to a normal brainstorm, for when a session gets stuck or keeps producing the same ideas: negative brainstorming, figuring storming and bodystorming. Which one fits depends on the problem, the people and the goal.
- **Use it to:** get many ideas in limited time · take other points of view and look at the problem from new angles · lift creativity again when a round goes in circles.
- **Why the book's expert picks it** (p. 169): in a normal brainstorm the focus drifts wide and the ideas stop touching the real problem. These techniques give another angle, and they take away the pressure of having to have a good idea.

## The three techniques (p. 168)

- **Negative brainstorming:** brainstorming turned around. Look for everything that would make the problem worse. The book's example: instead of easing traffic, work out how to make the jam as long as possible. Then read the results for new starting points, and for things that make the problem worse and could be removed.
- **Figuring storming:** a problem is often easier to solve from someone else's point of view. Ask how X would solve it. X can be a famous person, someone from everyday life (a partner, a family member, the boss) or a persona from the project.
- **Bodystorming:** put people physically into the situation, as close to real as possible, with the right surroundings, objects and people, so they can feel it and find ideas by trying. The book's example: a team designing for older people smears petroleum jelly on glasses to see the way they do. One variant: keep moving and write ideas down while walking.

Alone: negative brainstorming and figuring storming need nobody else. Bodystorming as written needs test persons and a built situation; its walking variant works for one person. (Claude's reading of p. 168)

## The cycle, alone

Reach for it when a normal round circles: the third round repeats the first.

1. **Name the technique.** Negative, figuring storming or walking. Write it down before the round.
2. **Run it short.** Stop when the ideas thin out. Short rounds, with no timer as a whip (Anna, 18.09.2026).
3. **Switch technique.** Switch instead of pushing. Still circling → back to 1 with the next technique. The circling stops → go on to 4.
4. **Cluster, then walk the matrix.** Only after the rounds: group what belongs together, then walk the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md) over the clusters.
5. **Clarity check.** All five pass → forward to the [2x2 matrix](../2x2-matrix/2x2-matrix.md). Not yet → back to 1 with another technique, or to the step an open check points at.

Stopping is allowed at any step.

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 169–170)

- **A creative setting:** people brainstorm best when relaxed; comfortable seats, cushions and large, flexible writing surfaces help. Alone: your own setting counts as a tool, and the sensory side is yours to arrange.
- **Timeboxing with a timer:** on Anna's no list (Anna, 18.09.2026). Short rounds yes, timer no; stop when the ideas thin out.
- **No judging during a session:** there are no bad ideas, and even an odd one may lead somewhere. Alone: the same rule as in brainstorming; judging happens at the matrix, never during a round.
- **For complex or abstract problems with no solution in sight;** experiment and gather experience. Alone: reach for it when a round circles.
- **Use case:** some teams felt early on that they were going in circles. Negative brainstorming widened their view, and in the figuring storming after it they borrowed the voices of big tech companies and a well-known entrepreneur, and found more approaches. Alone: the voices can be closer to home, such as a game designer, your earlier self, or whoever plays this later.
- **Key learnings:** it helps with complex or abstract problems · the usual rules hold: quantity before quality, everyone gets a turn, no bad ideas · only afterwards are the results clustered, rated and documented. Alone: quantity first, no judging while writing, then cluster and walk the matrix.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Brainstorming** (p. 151), an alternative: quantity before quality, with nine rules. [Brainstorming](../brainstorming/brainstorming.md).
- **Brainwriting 6-3-5** (p. 163), an alternative: six people each write three ideas and pass the sheet on five times, in silence. Not built here.
- **Analogies & benchmarking** (p. 171), an alternative: other worlds that solved the same pain. [Analogies & benchmarking](../analogies-and-benchmarking/analogies-and-benchmarking.md).
- **I like, I wish, I wonder** (p. 239), an alternative: a feedback ritual that keeps the mood good; it belongs to Reflect. Not built here.
- **Persona / user profile** (p. 97), a helper: figuring storming can borrow a persona's voice. Not built here. Default boxes are on Anna's no list; a persona is a real person, herself included (Anna, 18.09.2026).
- **Empathy map** (p. 93), a helper: what a person sees, hears, thinks and feels, says and does. Not built here (checked by Anna, not chosen, 19.09.2026).
