# 5W+H questions

**Support card** · used by the tools of the [design cycle](../../README.md) when a question is missing

> **AI-made.** Drafted by Claude (AI) from Anna (@annaothercrow)'s solo design-thinking toolkit, which adapts *The Design Thinking Toolbox* for one person. See [Who made what](#who-made-what) and [Sources](#sources).

Board version: [5w-h.excalidraw](5w-h.excalidraw). Open it in Excalidraw or in VS Code.

Six question words: who, what, when, where, why and how (Toolbox p. 71–74). The name is short for them: five W words, plus H for how. It is the one support that needs almost no change for one person, because the book itself says the size of the team is secondary (p. 72).

Skip any question that makes no sense where you are. That is the book's own instruction (p. 72–73).

## Two ways to use it (p. 72)

**1 · Understand the problem better.** The book: raise and answer every question that fits, skip the ones that don't, and look for where doubts or new questions show up. Alone: this is the way that feeds the [Problem statement](../../1-understand/problem-statement/problem-statement.md). The questions you can't answer are the ones worth a run.

**2 · Find the questions to watch for.** The book: gather sub-questions as a mind map, vary them, play with them, and build interview questions from them. Alone, the interview is with a record or with yourself. Three or four of the sub-questions can become what you watch for during the next run.

## The book, and the version for one person

| | The book (p. 72–73) | Alone |
|---|---|---|
| People | in a team, or to prepare an interview; the size of the team is secondary (p. 72) | Nothing to adapt. It works the same with one person as with five. |
| Time | 30–60 minutes; quick and light as a way in, or deep, with a lot of discussion (p. 72) | No fixed time. (Anna, 19.09.2026) Both ways are allowed. A two-minute pass to find the missing question is a full use. |
| Where answers come from | back them with facts, for example from desk research and data analysis (p. 73) | Your run log and your own records are the facts. |
| Materials | the book's template (p. 72) | This file or the board. The download template is behind a paywall and isn't used. |

"Alone" lines: Claude's suggestions, unless marked as Anna's.

## Expert tips, alone (p. 73)

- **Ask several times, with a beginner's mind, even when you think you know.** Alone: the second answer to the same question is often the honest one.
- **Find more than one answer to every question; answers that clash are the interesting ones.** Alone: two answers that disagree are a finding. Leave both standing.
- **Turn questions negative:** when does it not happen, who isn't affected? Alone: the way out when why runs dry. The [gentle why](../gentle-why/gentle-why.md) borrows it to go sideways instead of deeper.
- **Skip a question that makes no sense here.** Alone: the book's own permission to leave a box empty.
