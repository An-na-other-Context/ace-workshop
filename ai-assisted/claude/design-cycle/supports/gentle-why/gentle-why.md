# A gentle why

**Support card** · used by the tools of the [design cycle](../../README.md) that look for a cause

> **AI-made.** Drafted by Claude (AI) from Anna (@annaothercrow)'s solo design-thinking toolkit, which adapts *The Design Thinking Toolbox* for one person. The stop rule is Anna's (18.09.2026). See [Who made what](#who-made-what) and [Sources](#sources).

Board version: [gentle-why.excalidraw](gentle-why.excalidraw). Open it in Excalidraw or in VS Code.

The book's Ask 5x why (Toolbox p. 67–70), with one instruction turned around. You describe the problem, ask why, and write each answer down. The round ends when the cause shows, or at the first sign of discomfort.

**The flag.** The book's stop rule is to keep asking "until it gets uncomfortable" (p. 69, quoted), or until the real cause turns up. That rule is on Anna's no list (18.09.2026). Here, the first sign of discomfort ends the round. Same signal, opposite instruction: discomfort is never the target. This is the one line on the card that overrides the book. It is written here once, so it doesn't get argued again in every tool.

### 1 · Describe the problem

The book's first step, kept as it is: describe the problem in as much detail as you can (p. 68). A screenshot or a [sketch](../sketch/sketch.md) counts.

- The problem, in detail:
- My first guess at the cause (write it, then set it aside):

### 2 · Ask

Write each answer down before you ask the next why.

- Why ___________? Because ___________
- Why ___________? Because ___________
- Why ___________? Because ___________
- Why ___________? Because ___________
- Why ___________? Because ___________

Five is a direction, with no quota. The book asks for at least five (p. 70), and says in the same tool that five may not be enough (p. 69). Three whys that reach a cause beat seven that go in circles.

### 3 · Stop

The round ends at whichever comes first:

- [ ] the cause shows
- [ ] the first sign of discomfort
- [ ] nothing new after three whys → go sideways instead of deeper

Stopping is always allowed, for any reason.

**Going sideways** (the negative turn from [5W+H questions](../5w-h/5w-h.md), p. 73):

- When does it *not* happen?
- Who or what is *not* affected?
- Where was it solved before?

### 4 · Check it with the reverse question

Turn the answer into an if-then sentence and see whether it still holds (p. 69).

- If ___________ had not happened, then ___________ would not be the case.
- Does it hold? yes / partly / no
- If partly: what else was in it?

Holds → name the cause. Partly or no → you can go back to 2 with what else was in it, or stop here.

## The book, and the version for one person

| | The book (p. 68–70) | Alone |
|---|---|---|
| Who is in the room | pairs are ideal: one person leads the talk, the other documents (p. 68) | You ask. The [run log](../run-log/run-log.md) documents. The second person becomes a sheet. |
| Time | 30–40 minutes, the length of the interview (p. 68) | No fixed time. (Anna, 19.09.2026) It ends when the cause shows, or at the first sign of discomfort. |
| How many whys | at least five (p. 70); five may not be enough (p. 69) | Five is a direction. Three that reach a cause beat seven that circle. |
| When to stop | keep going until discomfort, or until the real cause shows (p. 69) | At the first sign of discomfort. (Anna, 18.09.2026, her no list) |
| Before you start | describe the problem in as much detail as you can, with photos or sketches (p. 68) | Kept as it is. A screenshot counts. |
| Materials | notepad and pen; a phone or camera if the person being interviewed agrees to a recording (p. 68) | The run log you already have open. Consent is yours to give. |

"Alone" lines: Claude's suggestions, unless marked as Anna's.

## Expert tips, alone (p. 69)

- **Make no assumptions about the roots of a problem.** Alone: write your first guess down before you start, then set it aside. Once it's named, it stops steering quietly.
- **Let the story be told, listen closely, ask when something is unclear.** Alone: let each answer finish before you answer it. Write it down before the next why.
- **Don't stop asking too soon.** Alone: keep this half and drop the push. If nothing new comes after three whys, go sideways instead of deeper.
- **The reverse question** (p. 69) is worth the extra minute. It catches a cause that only sounds right. Alone, nobody else is there to notice that it doesn't follow.

## The book's use case, and why it's a warning here (p. 70)

In the book's case, a team asks why so often that the person they interview stops answering, and the questions turn philosophical. In a group that reads as a story about persistence. Alone, the person who stops answering is you. That is a shutdown, and here it ends the round.

## The variation: 5x how (p. 68)

The book also suggests asking how, five times. Why reviews the problem; how looks for ways to solve it. Alone: why belongs in Understand and Reflect. How belongs in Ideate, once a [How might we](../../3-define/how-might-we/how-might-we.md) question exists. Same card, different phase.
