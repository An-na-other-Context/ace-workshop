The book has a whole page on sketch notes (Toolbox p. 174) and never lists it as a tool. It shows six building blocks, with nothing to learn. The page says it is meant for exactly the people who say they can't draw (p. 174).

A sketch is a container for a thought. If it holds the thought, it's finished.

### If the page is blank

1. **Container first.** What kind of thing is this: a thought, something said, a fact, a feeling? Draw that shape.
2. **Words inside it.**
3. **A figure, only if someone is doing something.**
4. **Stop when it holds the thought.** To check: would I understand it tomorrow? Not yet → back to 2 and add words.

Words alone are always fine. The sketch can come later, or not at all.

## The six building blocks (p. 174)

| Block | What the page shows |
|---|---|
| Forms | dot · line · square · triangle · circle. Nearly everything can be drawn from dots, lines and simple shapes. |
| Containers | the outline says what kind of thing is inside (table below) |
| Figures | start from a star: five strokes, then a head on top. That's a person. Several smaller ones make a group. |
| Faces | the shape of the eyes and mouth carries the feeling. Where the eyes sit shows where someone is looking. |
| Writing | a headline in three bands: one fifth, three fifths, one fifth. Letters set close together read as a title; spaced out, they read as notes. |
| Grey | grey around the outside makes a shape look flat. Grey inside makes it look solid. One marker, two effects. |

| Container | Holds |
|---|---|
| cloud | a thought |
| speech bubble | something said |
| rectangle with a tail | a factual statement |
| jagged bubble | something emotional |
| plain box | information |
| scroll, banner or arrow | a heading, a label, a direction |

The book compares putting the blocks together to putting letters into words, and words into stories (p. 174). None of it is a drawing skill: it's six shapes and where to put them.

Not reproduced here: the book's symbol row and its six page-layout patterns. They are on p. 174 itself.

## How it's used

- **Never the thing that stalls a step.** If drawing gets in the way, write the words and go on.
- **A screenshot counts** wherever the book says photos or sketches.
- **A lookup, with nothing to practise.** The book calls its page sketch note training. Here it gets opened when a box says draw.
- **Nobody else has to read it.** Readable to you tomorrow is the only test.
