> **AI-made.** Drafted by Claude (AI) from Anna (@annaothercrow)'s solo design-thinking toolkit, which adapts *The Design Thinking Toolbox* for one person. The choices are Anna's. See [Who made what](#who-made-what) and [Sources](#sources).

## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 207–209):

- **Goal:** turn needs into a simple working product, and find out whether it holds up.
- **What it is:** the smallest version that feels complete to the person using it and stays minimal in everything else. It moves between solving the whole problem (a functional prototype) and working out single details (an "X is finished" prototype).
- **Where it sits:** usually at a higher resolution (more detailed and finished) than the earlier prototypes. It is the step before something goes out into the world, a bit at a time.
- **What the results decide:** whether the MVP gets built on, changed, or dropped.
- **The template's fields** (p. 208): starting point (who it's for, the top three problems, the journey and the use cases) · vision and road map · top three features · build · costs and schedule · measure · learn · conclusion and next steps.
- **Why the book's expert picks it:** it calls for quick decisions and a focus on the core. It checks whether you are doing the right thing before you work on doing it right. It also keeps perfectionism in check.
- **In the cycle,** this is the far end of phase 5, used rarely (Anna, 20.09.2026). It is optional: look it up when a run needs it, and skip it when it doesn't (Anna, 20.09.2026).

Only half of the book's tool fits one person: the smallest thing that works. The market half is parked: see Parked, below.

## The cycle, alone

One MVP at a time, and only after small experiments already held (p. 208, 210).

1. **Starting point.** Who it's for, the top three problems, the part of the journey it improves, all from sheets you've already filled in. Which small experiments held, and what they showed. (book step 1)
2. **Vision and three features.** One sentence for where it's heading, and a rough road map. The core comes first. Walk the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md) to pick the three features to test next. (book steps 2–3)
3. **Build plan, and the measure first.** How it gets built, and how many sittings it should fit in: a size guess, never a deadline. Then, before building: how would you notice it worked, or didn't, and when is it enough? (book step 4)
4. **Clarity check.** All five pass → build it, use it in real runs, and record it on the [exploration map](../exploration-map/exploration-map.md). Not yet → back to the step the check points to. A missing measure, for example, goes back to 3.

↺ After a round of runs: write down what it taught, then improve one step (book steps 5–7). A run is an item on the [ACE Workshop board](https://github.com/orgs/An-na-other-Context/projects/1), logged on its issue.

No fixed time, and stopping is always allowed (Anna, 19.09.2026).

| 2 · Small experiments came first | The use case team reached a fit between problem and solution only after many rounds and many low-resolution prototypes, and only then widened it step by step (p. 210). |

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 209–210)

- **The 80/20 rule** (p. 209): about 80 percent of an offer can be shown with 20 percent of the building effort. Alone: ask which small fifth still shows the thing.
- **Low cost and little time** (p. 209) belong with solving the problem. Alone: if it needs weeks, it has grown into the project itself.
- **The standard case first** (p. 209), special cases later. The core of the idea is what counts. Alone: the standard case is an ordinary run on an ordinary day.
- **Small tests before building** (p. 209): show it to people who don't know it. If everyone asks the same question, the value isn't clear yet. Alone: show it to Claude. The same question twice is the signal.
- **"Fail fast, fail early, fail cheap"** (p. 209, quoted): it keeps perfectionism in check. Alone, in Anna's verdict: iterate often instead of fixing what doesn't work for you, and never "I failed" (Anna, 18.09.2026).
- **The use case** (p. 210): only after many rounds and many low-resolution prototypes did the team find a fit between problem and solution, and only then did they widen the functions step by step. They cut the offer down to something complete from the user's side and very small, tested one critical function, and held one last interview before building. Alone: the order matters, small experiments first. The critical function can come from the [critical items diagram](../../3-define/critical-items-diagram/critical-items-diagram.md).
- **Key learnings** (p. 210): use the template and test one MVP after another · always ask the user for feedback · iterate, iterate, iterate · fail early and cheaply. Alone: one at a time. You are the user, and a real run is the feedback.

"Alone" lines: Claude's suggestions, unless marked as Anna's.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Functional prototype** (p. 192), an alternative: one part of the vision that really works. On [Kinds of prototypes](../kinds-of-prototypes/kinds-of-prototypes.md).
- **"X is finished" prototype** (p. 193), an alternative: the detailed sub-functions. Also on [Kinds of prototypes](../kinds-of-prototypes/kinds-of-prototypes.md).
- **[Exploration map](../exploration-map/exploration-map.md)** (p. 195), a helper: where every experiment, expectation and result goes.
- **[Feedback capture grid](../../6-test/feedback-capture-grid/feedback-capture-grid.md)** (p. 217), a helper: four fields for what a test brought. Part of Test.
- **Structured usability test** (p. 229), a helper: a planned test with tasks. Not built here.
- **Minimum viable ecosystem** (p. 209), a variation: the same way of thinking applied to a whole business ecosystem with many actors. Parked, see below.

## Parked: the market half (p. 207–209)

The book's MVP also tests whether an offer will succeed on the market: whether it draws interest, how much demand exists before more gets built, and how to keep the money put into something nobody wants small. It covers the business model and costs in the business sense, and a variation, the minimum viable ecosystem, for digital products with many actors (p. 209). Alone, you are the market, so these wait. They come back the day something goes in front of other people.
