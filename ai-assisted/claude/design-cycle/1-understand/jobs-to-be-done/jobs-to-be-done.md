## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 75–77):

- **Goal:** see what adds value for a person and helps them get their tasks done.
- **The idea** (from Clayton Christensen): whenever people have a task to solve, they bring in a product or a service, as if they hired it for a job. The deeper social, emotional and personal jobs matter most.
- **The book's claim:** understand the tasks in depth, and success is close to certain. The job works like a compass.
- **Use it to:** capture tasks in an ordered way · find hidden tasks nobody named, and turn them into needs or sharpen known ones · improve a whole experience, for example to give a service a clear purpose.
- **One act, two jobs** (the book's example): editing a camera photo so it looks professional, to show a perfect picture; or editing a phone photo quickly, to share it with friends right away.
- **The book's steps** (p. 76): every job has three parts, the situation, the motivation and the expected result. Collect the tasks along the customer journey, each one on its own. Ask why for each task, and why not. Dig deeper with 5W+H questions (who, what, when, where, why, how) and by asking why five times. Then write each job as one sentence: *When I … (situation), I want to … (motivation), so I can … (expected result).*

## The cycle, alone

1. **Walk a moment.** One real moment, start to stop, task by task. Setup and stopping count too. A run is a good moment: an item on the [ACE Workshop board](https://github.com/orgs/An-na-other-Context/projects/1), logged on its issue (see the [run log](../../supports/run-log/run-log.md)).
2. **Write the jobs.** One per task: *When I · I want to · so I can*. Add a pain, a gain and the kind of job. Ask why, and why not, with the [gentle why](../../supports/gentle-why/gentle-why.md).
3. **Walk the matrix** over each job, out loud: the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md). What passes goes forward; the rest goes to the parking lot.
4. **Clarity check.** All five pass → done. Not yet → walk a second moment and write its jobs. The book asks for at least two interviews (p. 76).

No fixed time: short cycles, as for the [Problem statement](../../1-understand/problem-statement/problem-statement.md) (Anna, 19.09.2026). You can stop after any step; the bookmark keeps your place.

### 3 · Walk the matrix (out loud)

Ask each job these four questions:

1. A new job, or one I keep running into? → goes forward if: keeps coming back
2. Would getting this job done add value right now? → goes forward if: yes
3. Can I get this job done with what I have? → goes forward if: doable

## The five clarity checks, and where they come from

| Check | What the book says |
|---|---|
| 1 · Three parts | Every job has three elements: the situation, the motivation and the expected result. (p. 76) |
| 2 · No solution in the result | The expected result says precisely what the person wants to achieve, and it doesn't depend on any solution. (p. 77) |
| 3 · One job per sentence | Write each task on its own and keep tasks apart; each one gets measured separately. (p. 76, 77) |
| 4 · A hidden job is named | The hidden tasks, the non-functional, social, emotional or personal ones, matter most and are often the key. (p. 78) |
| 5 · Clear to me tomorrow | Carried over from the Problem statement: everybody on the team must understand the problem (p. 28). Alone, the "team" is you on another day. (Claude's wording) |

Checks 1–4 were proposed by Claude. Anna kept the sheet with all five (19.09.2026).

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 77–78)

- **Ask about tasks to find needs.** People find it hard to talk about their needs, and easier to talk about their tasks and where they got stuck. Alone: describe what you did and where it got hard. The need shows up in the "so I can".
- **Look below the surface.** Besides the core task, look at the tasks next to it, the emotional ones, and every touch point. Alone: walk the whole moment, start to stop. Setup and stopping count.
- **Keep tasks apart.** That's clarity check 3. The book measures each task with KPIs; those are parked below.
- **Keep the result free of solutions.** That's clarity check 2. The book checks results with surveys; those are parked below.
- **Note pains and gains** for each task: the troubles, and the benefits or joys. Alone: a pain line and a gain line under every job.
- **Workarounds tell a lot.** They show where people put their time and money. Alone: your own workarounds count as data. The sheet has a line for them.
- **The use case** collects the tasks along the journey, then adds the emotional and social ones: "Don't forget the emotional and social tasks" (p. 78, quoted). After prioritising, the most important become jobs, with their pains and gains. Alone: walk your moment step by step, then ask which emotional, social or personal job is missing. The matrix does the prioritising.
- **Key learnings** (p. 78): the three-part sentence · the hidden jobs matter most and are often the key to the solution · use the frame for every task along the journey. Here: the sheet's sentence, clarity check 4, and every task of your moment.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Persona / user profile** (p. 97), an alternative: a made-up character standing for a type of user. Alone the user is you, so no stand-in is needed. Its default boxes, such as gender and demographics, are on Anna's no list (18.09.2026). Not built here.
- **5W+H questions** (p. 71), a helper: [5W+H](../../supports/5w-h/5w-h.md). The book uses the six question words to dig into indirect goals and tasks.
- **Ask 5x why** (p. 67–69), a helper: asking why again and again to reach the real cause. Here it is the [gentle why](../../supports/gentle-why/gentle-why.md). The book's tip to keep asking "until it gets uncomfortable" (p. 69, quoted) is on Anna's no list (18.09.2026). Her version: stop at the first sign of discomfort, or ask when the problem does *not* happen.
- **Interview for empathy** (p. 57), a helper: see the world from the person's side, early, before any solution. Alone, the interview is you reading your own records. Not built here.
- **Customer journey map** (p. 103), a helper: [Customer journey map](../../2-observe/customer-journey-map/customer-journey-map.md). The book collects jobs along a journey. Alone, your journey is one real moment, such as a run.

## Parked: KPIs, surveys and interview pairs (p. 76–77)

The book's expert measures each task with KPIs (numbers that show progress) set beforehand, and checks the expected result with quantitative surveys, questionnaires that many people answer (p. 77). Both measure a market of many customers. With one person there is no survey to run. Interviews in pairs, one asking and one writing (p. 76), come back when there are other people; for now, your own records stand in. What stays: decide beforehand how you would notice that a job got done. That belongs to the Test phase.
