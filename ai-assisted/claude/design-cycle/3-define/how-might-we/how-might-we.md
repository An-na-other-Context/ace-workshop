## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 125–127):

- **Goal:** a question that makes focused work in Ideate possible.
- **Why these three words:** *how* says there is more than one way. *Might* makes a safe space, where an idea may work or may not. *We* says it gets solved together.
- **The shape:** How might we + a verb + a noun + the kind of user it's for.
- **The test:** read it aloud. Too narrow: it already holds a solution, or leaves nothing to explore. Too broad: it tries to improve the world, and you feel lost in front of it. Asking *why* widens the focus; asking *how* narrows it.
- **Then:** ideation starts, for example with an open brainstorm.
- **Why the book's expert uses it** (Andrés Bedoya, d.school Paris, a design school): it joins a deep understanding of people with the open field of ideas in Ideate.

## The cycle, alone

1. **Gather the findings** from the [problem statement](../../1-understand/problem-statement/problem-statement.md), the [jobs](../../1-understand/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done.md), the [AEIOU](../../2-observe/aeiou/aeiou.md) pattern (activities, environments, interactions, objects, users), the [customer journey map](../../2-observe/customer-journey-map/customer-journey-map.md), and the [critical items diagram](../../3-define/critical-items-diagram/critical-items-diagram.md) if you have one.
2. **Write three or four** questions in the shape: How might we + a verb + a noun + who it's for.
3. **Test each one with ideas.** Write ideas for about five minutes per question, without judging them. Ideas come → the question fits. Nothing comes → it's off. The five minutes give the test its size; nobody is timing you. A question that's off gets reshaped: too narrow → ask why it should be that way, and use the answer. Too broad → ask how it would work, and use the answer.
4. **Matrix, then the clarity check.** Walk the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md) out loud over the questions that fit. Then check. All five pass → Ideate can start. Not yet → widen with why, narrow with how, and test again.

No fixed time, and no time pressure for its own sake (Anna, 18.09.2026). The idea test comes before the matrix, so the matrix works on evidence instead of a hunch.

**If a question is off**

- Too broad (it tries to fix the whole world) → ask **how** it would work, and use the answer.

### 4 · Walk the matrix (out loud), over the questions that fit

Ask each one these four questions, from the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md):

1. A new question, or one that keeps coming back? → goes forward if: keeps coming back
2. Would answering it add value right now? → goes forward if: yes
3. Can I work on it with what I have? → goes forward if: doable
4. Does it get costlier if it stays unanswered? → goes forward if: costlier

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 127–128)

- **No right or wrong; let the gut decide whether a question fits.** The gut is on Anna's no list (Anna, 18.09.2026). The signal the expert describes stays: if ideas come, it fits.
- **Prepare several questions for a subject.** Each one is a prototype, tested in a short brainstorm. Alone: three or four, about five minutes of ideas each.
- **Name the person as well as the problem**, and bring them and their need forward as soon as the problem is known. Alone: your question names you, and the need from your problem statement.
- **Be bold and visible:** a thick marker, a large sheet, sticky notes. Alone: put the chosen question where you'll see it while ideating.
- **The use case** (p. 128): everyone prepares questions, and the team shapes them with how and why until they agree on the right one. Lilly gives this enough time, because the right question makes the next phase faster. Alone: you shape them with how and why yourself, and saying them out loud counts as the discussion. Enough time, in short cycles.
- **Key learnings** (p. 128): keep the discussion of a question short, since time pressure keeps you agile · stay optimistic and close to the user's needs. Alone: time pressure is on Anna's no list (Anna, 18.09.2026), so the idea test is the stop rule, with no clock. Stay close to your own needs, as your jobs describe them.
