> **AI-made.** Drafted by Claude (AI) from Anna (@annaothercrow)'s solo design-thinking toolkit, which adapts *The Design Thinking Toolbox* for one person. The choices are Anna's. See [Who made what](#who-made-what) and [Sources](#sources).

Board version: [aeiou.excalidraw](aeiou.excalidraw). Open it in Excalidraw or in VS Code.

## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 107–109):

- **Goal:** learn more about the problem, the person and their surroundings.
- **What it is:** a frame for observing, made by Rick Robinson, Ilya Prokopoff, John Cain and Julie Pokorny in 1991. AEIOU stands for its five letters: Activities · Environment · Interactions · Objects · Users. It uses W+H questions (who, what, when, where, why, how) to reach deep insights.
- **Where it's used:** mostly in Understand and Observe; also as a source of ideas.
- **Use it to:** give an observation a structure and ask the questions that matter · sort many findings from observations made side by side · relate a person to an activity, a space and an object · collect insights that aren't public · give people new to observing a frame to hold on to.
- **The book's steps** (p. 108): find out where and when the person can be found · go there, into the context of the problem · observe with the template's questions, taking notes, photos or videos · afterwards, sort the notes under the five letters, then cluster them into themed blocks with headings, so a pattern shows.
- **On yourself:** the book's own "design thinking life" uses AEIOU on your own records (p. 109, 289–291).

## The cycle, alone

One observation per cycle.

1. **Pick a moment.** A run, or part of one, and how you'll keep notes. A run is an item on the [ACE Workshop board](https://github.com/orgs/An-na-other-Context/projects/1), logged on its issue; its [run log](../../supports/run-log/run-log.md) is the record to read. Notes taken during or right after a moment count too.
2. **Notes under A E I O U.** The five letters' questions, asked about yourself. Write what you did and what you used. The "why" waits for the pattern.
3. **Sort into blocks.** After two or more observations: themed blocks with a heading each, then one pattern that holds across them.
4. **Clarity check.** All five pass → done. Not yet → observe another moment, then sort again.

No fixed time (Anna, 19.09.2026). Keep the notes short. Stopping when it gets heavy is always allowed.

No matrix walk in this tool: sorting isn't picking, so nothing needs criteria (see where the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md) does not apply).

## The five letters, asked about yourself

The book's template questions are for watching someone else. Here they are reworded about yourself. The book's expert says to adapt them (p. 109).

| Letter | The book asks (p. 108) | About yourself |
|---|---|---|
| A · Activities | What happens? What are the people doing, and what is their task? What happens before and after? | What did I do? What was my task? What came before and after? Which activities did I enjoy? |
| E · Environment | What does the space look like? What is its nature and its function? | Where was I? What did the desk, screen and room look like? What was the space good or bad for? |
| I · Interactions | How do the systems work together, and are there interfaces? How do the users deal with each other? How does the operation work? | How did I move between things: book, files, Claude? Where did I switch, and where did it snag? |
| O · Objects | Which objects and devices are used, by whom, and where? | What did I use? |
| U · Users | Who are the users? What role do they play? Who influences them? | Who was there: me, Claude, the voice of the book? Who influenced what I did? |

## The five clarity checks, and where they come from

| Check | What the book says |
|---|---|
| 1 · All five letters have notes | Work through the five areas of the template. (p. 108) |
| 2 · Seen, not guessed | The frame gives structure to what was observed and recorded. Here the notes hold what happened and what was used; reasons wait for the pattern. (p. 108) |
| 3 · Sorted into themed blocks | Cluster and sort the findings into themed blocks, each with a heading that sums it up. (p. 108) |
| 4 · A pattern named | The blocks are there so a pattern can show; the five categories are linked. (p. 108, 109) |
| 5 · Clear to me tomorrow | Anna chose this check for Problem statement and Jobs to be done (19.09.2026). It goes back to the book's rule that everybody on the team must understand the problem (p. 28). Alone, the "team" is you on another day. (Claude's wording) |

Checks 1–4 were proposed by Claude. Anna kept the sheet with all five (20.09.2026).

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 109–110)

- **A starting point, open to change.** Adapt the questions to what you need. The five letters are useful categories, not a rigid frame. Here the questions are reworded about yourself.
- **The book's own solo use.** In design thinking life, AEIOU looks back at the records of an energy journal, and the activities ask about yourself. Here your run records are the journal.
- **Sub-categories for sequences.** For complex problems, split a letter, especially when things happen in order. The categories are closely linked. Alone: if a moment has a clear order, split Activities into before, during and after.
- **Show, don't tell.** Drawings of a sequence and storytelling share the bigger picture. Alone: a quick [sketch](../../supports/sketch/sketch.md) of the moment is enough.
- **The use case** (p. 110): the five headings make results from different observations easy to join, the steps already hold the main W+H questions, and an observation can happen on the spot, with little preparation. Alone: two or more observations, joined by the same five headings. Any moment can be observed, unplanned.
- **Key learnings** (p. 110): AEIOU helps field research, for inspiration and a basic knowledge of a problem, and gives newcomers a frame whose results are quick to join. Alone: a basic knowledge of your own moments, with a structure that asks few decisions of you.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Design thinking life** (p. 289–291), the book's solo use: design thinking for your own life, with two added steps, acceptance and self-reflection, mixed with coaching and systemic therapy. It names AEIOU as a helper. Not built here.
- **Explorative interview** (p. 63), a helper: learn about people's everyday life, values, motives and unspoken needs before thinking about products. It names AEIOU as its alternative. Not built here.
- **5W+H questions** (p. 71), a helper: [5W+H](../../supports/5w-h/5w-h.md). Open questions to capture a situation in an ordered way.
- **Ask 5x why** (p. 67–69), a helper: asking why again and again to reach the real cause. Here it is the [gentle why](../../supports/gentle-why/gentle-why.md). The book's tip to keep asking "until it gets uncomfortable" (p. 69, quoted) is on Anna's no list (18.09.2026); her version stops at the first sign of discomfort.
- **Persona / user profile** (p. 97), a helper: a made-up user. Alone the user is you. Its default boxes are on Anna's no list (18.09.2026). Not built here.
- **Other frames** the book lists without pages: Spradley's nine dimensions (space, actors, activities, objectives, acts, events, time, goals, feelings) · A×4 (atmosphere, actors, activities, artifacts) · Sotirin (territory, people, stuff, talk) · POSTA (person, objects, situation, time, activity). Named only. Not built here.
