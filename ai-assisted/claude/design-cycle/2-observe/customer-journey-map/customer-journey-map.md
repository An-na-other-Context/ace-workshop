## What it's for

The book's description, in other words (Toolbox p. 103–105):

- **Goal:** walk in someone's shoes, to understand in detail what they go through with a company, a product or a service.
- **What it is:** a map of a person's actions, thoughts and feelings across an interaction. It follows the person; a process map follows the internal steps. It takes in the steps around the product too: finding out, waiting, ordering, setting up, asking for help, throwing away.
- **Where it's used:** in Understand, Observe and Prototype. It is also the base for a service blueprint.
- **Use it to:** build a shared picture of someone's experience · find the "moments of misery" (p. 103, quoted) · see every touch point · close gaps · design a better experience · keep improving it over time.
- **The book's expert** (p. 105): seeing the process from the person's side brings sudden insights. Use tools to fit the purpose, from principles, in no fixed order.
- **The book's steps** (p. 104): 1 choose a persona · 2 choose a scenario or a job to be done · 3 mark every step before, during and after · 4 place the actions · 5 and 6 add what the person thinks and feels at each step, which draws the emotion curve · 7 and 8 name the chances to improve, and who is responsible for each.

## The cycle, alone

One phase of the journey per cycle.

1. **Moment and job.** You are the person on the map. A job from your [Jobs to be done](../../1-understand/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done.md) sheet is the scenario. Write down your goal for the moment. The moment can be a run: an item on the [ACE Workshop board](https://github.com/orgs/An-na-other-Context/projects/1), logged on its issue; its [run log](../../supports/run-log/run-log.md) gives the steps.
2. **Map one phase.** Before, during or after: action, thinking and feeling at each step. Then draw the curve.
3. **Walk the matrix** over the low points of the curve, out loud: the [discernment matrix](../../supports/discernment-matrix/discernment-matrix.md). Name a chance and who could change it.
4. **Clarity check.** All five pass → done. Not yet → map the next phase.

No fixed time (Anna, 19.09.2026). Stopping between phases is always allowed; the bookmark keeps your place.

## The five clarity checks, and where they come from

| Check | What the book says |
|---|---|
| 1 · Before, during, after | Define what happens before, during and after, so the most important steps are in. (p. 104) |
| 2 · Action, thought, feeling at every step | Add what the person thinks and feels at each step. (p. 104) |
| 3 · Moments of misery named | The map finds the "moments of misery" that hurt the experience. (p. 103, quoted) |
| 4 · Every chosen problem has an owner | Step 8 names who is responsible for each action. Alone: you, Claude, a page or tool. (p. 104) |
| 5 · Clear to me tomorrow | Anna chose this check for Problem statement and Jobs to be done (19.09.2026). It goes back to the book's rule that everybody on the team must understand the problem (p. 28). Alone, the "team" is you on another day. (Claude's wording) |

## Expert tips and use case, alone (p. 105–106)

- **Ask like an investigative journalist,** with questions that deepen the emotional journey. Alone: ask yourself [5W+H](../../supports/5w-h/5w-h.md) questions (who, what, when, where, why, how) at each step: what happened, when, why.
- **Context matters.** Newlyweds buying their first home go through a different journey from a millionaire buying a holiday home. Alone: name the context of your moment: which day, which energy, which task.
- **What a useful journey holds:** all actions, the context, the thinking behind a decision, the feeling afterwards, every chance to improve. Alone: the same lines, for your moment.
- **A journey can be a first prototype.** Actions, goals, expectations and failures can shape a future experience. Alone: a journey of how a run should go can become a prototype later.
- **Ways to improve:** take out negative moments or offer help earlier, link physical and digital, strengthen good moments, change the order. Alone: the same list, for your chance line.
- **The use case** (p. 106): the team agrees on the goals, splits the journey into main phases and looks at each on its own. The map and the curve then show the major problem areas, and new insights come up in the talk. Alone: one phase per cycle; write down the moment and your goal before starting; the matrix sets the problem areas, and saying them out loud is the talk.
- **Key learnings** (p. 106): capturing feelings along a journey builds a shared understanding of a person and their problems · touch points, where the person meets the company, can be improved one by one. Alone: an understanding of your own moment, with your feelings in it. Your touch points: the book, the plan, Claude, your files.

## Tools the book names alongside it

- **Service blueprint** (p. 203), an alternative: adds what happens behind the scenes (back-office steps, supporting processes, technology, data) and sets quality and time measures for each interaction. Not built here.
- **Interview for empathy** (p. 57), a helper: see the world from the person's side, early in the cycle: needs, feelings, motives, and the mental model behind how they work. Not built here.
- **Persona / user profile** (p. 97), a helper: the book's map starts with one. Alone the person is you. Default boxes such as gender and age are on Anna's no list (18.09.2026). Not built here.
- **Jobs to be done** (p. 75), a helper: [Jobs to be done](../../1-understand/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done.md). *When I … I want to … so I can …*, with pains and gains. Anna kept it (19.09.2026); a job from its sheet can be the map's scenario.
